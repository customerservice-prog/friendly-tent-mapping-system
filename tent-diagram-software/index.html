What a useful tent diagram includes.

Start with the tent’s size and style. Add each table, the intended seating and the equipment that occupies floor space. A dance floor, beverage station or service table belongs in the first draft, because adding it later can change the entire seating arrangement.

Show the space between the objects.

The blank parts of a diagram matter. People need routes from the entrance to their seats and to service areas. Keep central poles and other obstructions visible. Share the drawing with the rental team so they can review clearances and the actual installation conditions.

Compare the overhead plan with a 3D view.

An overhead diagram is useful for placement, but many customers find a perspective view easier to understand. RentSketch lets you see the same event in both. The product demo shows how the relationship between tables, seating and the tent changes from another viewpoint.

Use the diagram in a customer conversation.

For rental companies, a diagram is a starting point for a quote discussion. Configure supported equipment in your catalog and bring customer plans into your dashboard. Review the design alongside availability, site access and delivery details before accepting a rental order.

Keep a version everyone can refer to.

An individual Event Pass provides 30 days of editing, saving, printing and sharing one design. Use a saved plan for discussion, and communicate changes when the event details move. Software access does not include rental equipment or approval of a tent installation.
