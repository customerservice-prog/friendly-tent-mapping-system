Start with the footprint, not just the guest count.

A guest count is a starting point. The arrangement of tables, service areas and a dance floor changes how the event uses a tent. Begin with the tent’s dimensions and style, then identify what has to fit inside. A pole tent also introduces interior poles that need to remain visible in the planning conversation.

Build the event around the equipment.

Add the table types and chair arrangements you intend to use. Include the DJ, buffet, bar and other equipment before calling the plan complete. Reserve circulation space and review entrances instead of filling every available area with seating. RentSketch’s visual plan helps show those tradeoffs before setup day.

Use 2D for arrangement and 3D for perspective.

The overhead view makes it easier to compare positions. The 3D view gives customers a different way to understand the same design. Review both when discussing what the event will look like. You can explore a furnished example without signing up on the product demo page.

Review the real site before confirming the rental.

A diagram is a planning aid. Actual ground conditions, tent anchoring, exits and installation clearances must be checked with the rental team and venue. Manufacturer instructions and local requirements determine the installation; an attractive digital plan does not approve it.

Bring the plan into the quote.

Rental businesses can configure products and prices, map supported visuals and review customer quote requests. Confirm availability and final service details in your rental workflow. Individual planners can purchase a separate Event Pass when they need to edit, save and share their own event.
